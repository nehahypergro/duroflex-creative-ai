# Image format treatment playbook

Version 1.4.0 · reviewed 2026-10-29

Format treatments guide the strategist, creative director, copy, art and review stages. The brief determines the message; the selected destination determines the treatment. These recommendations allow justified creative alternatives. Exact placement specifications, client requirements and same-brand product evidence remain controlling.

A mapped playbook means guidance can be selected, not that every named deliverable has a working renderer or has passed visual review. Amazon image presets, RCS, push and selected local/retail/print formats have dedicated route mappings. Exact physical formats select their own treatment below. Unmapped destinations retain placement rules and an explicit treatment-coverage gap. Printer templates, installed screen/player acceptance and live artwork quality are separate proofs. Batch store/language production, dealer toolkit assembly and catalogue production remain separate workflows.

## Shared handoffs

- **Strategist:** State the communication job, audience/context assumption, main proposition, evidence, omitted content and why this destination changes the approach.
- **Creative director:** Choose meaningful concept alternatives within the selected treatment; do not turn a recommendation into one fixed look.
- **Copywriter and art director:** Agree native versus embedded text, necessary qualifiers, hierarchy, source imagery, crop and scene before rendering. Revise optional content or composition if it cannot fit.
- **Generation:** Use the selected concept and pinned same-brand references; preserve exact product identity and original technology artwork.
- **Review:** Check actual artwork at the intended size and in sequence. Record observed defects, unknowns and justified creative departures separately.
- **Delivery:** Reconcile required children and selected versions. Native fields, website components, print proofs and publisher acceptance are distinct from a saved image.

## Common rules

- **brief-first (contract):** Use the selected brand and exact SKU/variant only. The official website is client-approved for its actual technical/CGI/certification/warranty statements; preserve issuer, scope, dates and qualifiers. Source approval is not output approval. All commercial offers come from the current brief. Reuse original technology artwork without reconstruction when depicting technical illustrations. When the selected owned-pdp v4 contract permits a sourced exterior-view alternative and omission of unresolved warranty/replacement claims, follow that slot-specific policy; do not require optional illustrations or claims to proceed. Do not introduce a standalone campaign master KV deliverable. Standing testing policy for both brands: source acceptance remains active until the user explicitly reinstates source-verification blocking. Do not block or fail solely for absent independent verification or repeat approval of the supplied website/brief statements. Do not expire this policy after a run or silently tighten it on retry. Generated artwork defects, invented claims and actual required source contradictions remain separate checks.
- **format-intent (recommendation):** Before concepts, explain the selected format’s communication job, viewing context, one main proposition, supporting evidence, omitted content, native-versus-embedded copy, visual approach and action. Explain why the same brief changes treatment across destinations. Audience behaviour without evidence is a working hypothesis, not measured insight.
- **creative-freedom (recommendation):** Treat playbooks as decision support, not fixed templates. Choose imagery, people, setting, lighting and graphic language from the brief and same-brand guidance. No default beige room, fixed photo box, mandatory people, blanket no-people rule or mandatory headline/body/CTA stack. Vary creative routes meaningfully while preserving facts and message.
- **copy-layout-negotiation (contract):** Allocate copy roles and readable space together before rendering. If the content cannot fit, shorten optional copy, change composition or request an allowed extra module; never drop locked wording or qualifiers or shrink required text below useful reading size. OCR transcription alone cannot prove legibility.
- **quality-evidence (recommendation):** Review the actual output in its intended container, crop, viewing size and sequence. Check message clarity, identity, source fidelity, required wording, legibility, action and delivery completeness. Distinguish observed defects from preferences; unknown is not pass. Only applicable client/platform contracts can block as requirements; recommendations guide critique and allow justified alternatives.
- **scope-and-delivery (contract):** A treatment definition does not enable a renderer, publisher integration or native document format. Preserve missing-family/input limitations. Two options means two independently complete reviewed sets when requested; do not average away a failed child. Preserve versions, records and costs; no publishing or paid generation is authorized by this library.
- **client-brief-source-authority (contract):** The current user-authored brief is client-approved source authority for explicitly supplied offer/price/festive/EMI/bank terms and attributed expert proof or quotes, including their supplied approval/verification. Do not request separate independent verification or client reapproval solely for using those exact statements. Client-brief evidence records preserve complete supplied text and source provenance; they are not independent authentication. Preserve conditions, attribution, eligibility, dates and qualifiers. Do not invent missing values or imply a quote proves an unstated clinical outcome. Actual contradictions, absent required information, partner artwork availability and rendered errors remain separate checks. The exact-SKU official website is final client-approved source authority for technical/CGI, certifications, warranty and product statements; independent proof alone is not a prerequisite across formats.
- **editable-text-design-handoff (recommendation):** For the editable-text workflow, strategy chooses the message and art direction reserves purposeful space on a real backdrop for the agreed hierarchy. Generate the scene without added campaign text; add agreed wording through editable layers afterward. Preserve genuine product markings and source technical artwork. Native titles/buttons stay in native fields. Review the final composite for copy fit, contrast, product occlusion and reading order. Image-only work may have no overlay; do not require a headline, CTA or emotional appeal merely to fill a layout.

## Social

### Instagram / Facebook feed

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Make one proposition understandable during scrolling. Choose launch, benefit, occasion or proof from the brief; say why that proposition matters to the supplied audience.

**Copy treatment.** A concise headline may carry the idea; optional support must add evidence, not restate it. Keep a product identifier when recognition needs it. Put elaboration in the native caption, but never move an essential qualifier away from its claim.

**Scene and composition.** Choose product-led, human-use, editorial or contextual imagery for the message. Let one focal relationship dominate. Square needs compact hierarchy; portrait may give the product more context, not automatically more copy.

**Action and native fields.** Choose one realistic next step. Organic posts can invite useful discussion or discovery; paid posts align the image promise with native CTA and landing page. A visual button is not an interactive control.

**Sequence and options.** Each image is complete on its own. Two options explore different storytelling premises and meaningful composition choices while preserving the same proposition and facts.

**Required inputs.** Exact channel, organic/paid context, selected canvas, intended preview, brief audience/objective and scoped product references. Do not infer audience from the platform alone.

**What to review.** Can the viewer identify what is being shown and the main point at normal feed width? Check required copy, qualifiers, product markings, crop and native-caption consistency.

**What to avoid.** Avoid feature dumps, arbitrary headline/body/CTA quotas, invented urgency, tiny legal text and stretching one landscape composition into portrait.

**Selected variants**

- **square:** Use a compact focal composition; do not shrink a wide multi-column design.
- **portrait:** Recompose for vertical inspection; protect product silhouette and preview crop.
- **landscape:** Short displayed height reduces copy capacity; put secondary explanation in native text.

### Instagram / Facebook Story

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Communicate one immediate takeaway in a full-screen, tap-through context. Decide whether the frame introduces, demonstrates, reminds or asks; a static frame cannot rely on motion to explain it.

**Copy treatment.** Prefer one short thought with only essential support. The viewer should understand it without stopping to decode a paragraph. Retain required qualifiers visibly; reduce the claim or request another frame if they cannot fit.

**Scene and composition.** Compose vertically from the start. Choose a purposeful product crop or complete view according to recognition needs; closer framing is a choice, not a rule. Reserve real scene space for the message and actual platform overlays.

**Action and native fields.** Name the intended interaction: link sticker, native paid CTA, reply or no action. Reserve its area using the supplied preview/template. Do not paint a fake working sticker into the asset.

**Sequence and options.** For a requested series, assign distinct context, evidence and action beats. Each frame should remain intelligible if adjacent frames are skipped. Do not generate extra frames without scope approval.

**Required inputs.** Organic versus paid, final interface preview, requested frame count, interaction destination and exact source assets. Use secondary safe-area guidance provisionally when official access is blocked.

**What to review.** Inspect on a phone with header/footer/action overlays. Check time-to-understand qualitatively, copy legibility, crop, product identity and whether the action remains usable.

**What to avoid.** Avoid a feed poster centered in a tall empty canvas, dense text, automatic timers/urgency and universal safe-area percentages treated as verified policy.

### Social carousel / connected slides

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Choose a sequence because the story needs progression, comparison or explanation. State the reader’s question and why multiple slides help.

**Copy treatment.** The opening promises a specific useful takeaway; each subsequent slide adds a new fact, contrast or step. Use short signposts and retain local qualifiers on the slide carrying the claim.

**Scene and composition.** Create a consistent visual system with intentional rhythm: vary scale, framing or information structure as the story changes. Do not repeat the same product hero on every page without a distinct purpose.

**Action and native fields.** Close with a useful synthesis and an appropriate next step. Do not put identical sales CTAs on every slide. Native captions and paid card destinations are separate fields.

**Sequence and options.** Plan ordered page roles before rendering: hook/context → distinct explanation/evidence → synthesis/action as appropriate. Page count comes from the brief. Store one collection per concept with ordered children.

**Required inputs.** Requested count, platform/publishing mode, sequence purpose, evidence per claim, cover preview and any individual card destinations.

**What to review.** Review slides individually and as a set: missing/duplicate pages, progression, contradiction, continuity, numbering and cover-to-final promise fulfilment. Partial sets cannot pass whole-set review.

**What to avoid.** Avoid resize-only slides, filler to reach the count, unsupported before/after claims, orphan qualifiers and confusing carousel ads with native document posts.

### LinkedIn feed image

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Choose the relevant professional or corporate angle from the brief: product launch, company news, partner update, useful insight or employer story. LinkedIn does not automatically turn a consumer mattress into a B2B proposition.

**Copy treatment.** Use an informative headline and a small amount of evidence where needed. Keep introductory/native text separate. Prefer a precise insight over a slogan or corporate jargon; preserve names, roles and source wording.

**Scene and composition.** Use a credible focal product, person, chart or scene that makes the point visible. Keep typography comfortable in the selected landscape/square/portrait preview; tall imagery does not imply desktop eligibility.

**Action and native fields.** One action consistent with the objective and native destination. Awareness need not force a purchase button; an announcement can invite reading the actual source.

**Sequence and options.** Single images stand alone; requested variants preserve the same facts but recompose. Long explanations should be separately scoped as documents/carousels rather than squeezed into the graphic.

**Required inputs.** Audience supplied by brief, corporate facts, actual channel/device context, reference imagery and selected placement contract.

**What to review.** Check headline comprehension, proof accuracy, readable support/CTA, actual product markings, native-copy consistency and preview on each intended device.

**What to avoid.** Avoid dense presentation slides, tiny footer proof, generic boardroom imagery unrelated to the brief, and treating platform character limits as embedded-image copy targets.

### LinkedIn document / slide publication

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Develop a useful argument or learning resource that rewards advancing through pages. Specify what the reader will understand or decide by the end.

**Copy treatment.** Make the cover promise concrete. Give each page one argument with evidence; prefer plain headings, short explanations and labelled charts. Keep citations close to their claims.

**Scene and composition.** Use a readable page grid and consistent section markers. Design for page viewing on a phone as well as the downloadable document; avoid dense slide-deck screenshots.

**Action and native fields.** Close with a relevant next step and source/contact information when supplied. Gated access and lead forms belong to native publishing setup.

**Sequence and options.** Outline cover, context, evidence/application and conclusion; verify reading order, completeness, references and page numbers across the document.

**Required inputs.** Authored source content, audience, approved facts, requested page count, native document profile and digital accessibility needs.

**What to review.** Inspect all pages at intended preview width, selectable text/accessibility when supported, hyperlinks and exported page order. Raster slides alone do not establish an accessible PDF.

**What to avoid.** Avoid inventing research, using fonts below readable size to hit a page quota, and calling a set of PNGs a fully editable document.

### YouTube thumbnail

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Represent the actual linked video with an immediately recognisable visual promise. Curiosity must be resolved by the video, not invented drama.

**Copy treatment.** Use optional concise text that complements the video title. Avoid copying the whole title into the picture or adding unsupported comparisons.

**Scene and composition.** Use a strong subject relationship and intelligible silhouette at thumbnail size. Keep key details away from actual interface overlays such as duration. Preserve faces/product identity.

**Action and native fields.** The real action is opening the video. Do not simulate extra playback controls or claim results absent from the video.

**Sequence and options.** Each thumbnail is a complete alternative for the same video; keep experiments distinct without altering the factual promise.

**Required inputs.** Actual video topic/title, authentic stills or permitted product imagery, target platform profile and overlay preview.

**What to review.** Review tiny feed preview and full export; verify content promise, identity, crop, file specification and non-misleading treatment.

**What to avoid.** Avoid applying Community-post assumptions or inventing testimony, reactions and results.

### Pinterest image / Pin

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Offer an idea worth saving or exploring: a use, setting, product solution or practical guide. The Pin should remain understandable outside the original posting moment.

**Copy treatment.** Add an informative text cue only when it clarifies the idea. Keep detailed discovery terms and description in native fields; avoid unreadable keyword stuffing in artwork.

**Scene and composition.** Make the subject/product easy to recognise in a vertical browsing grid. Let useful setting details support the idea while preserving hierarchy and actual product appearance.

**Action and native fields.** Match the destination to the depicted idea/product. Native product tags and links are separate; do not draw fake shopping controls.

**Sequence and options.** Each Pin is self-contained; collections may explore different practical ideas, not duplicate a single crop.

**Required inputs.** Destination, idea/topic, product source and selected organic/paid profile. Do not extrapolate one Pin type’s rules to all others.

**What to review.** Check grid-preview readability, subject visibility, brand/product recognition and promise-to-destination agreement.

**What to avoid.** Avoid noisy collages without a clear organising idea, unnecessary overlay text and false claims of guaranteed saves or clicks.

**Sequence and options.** For a carousel give each card a distinct contribution and useful opening. Standalone art should not rely on an unseen next card.

## Paid media

### PMax / responsive display asset set

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Define one coherent product/audience theme per asset group. Provide complementary visual routes that can work with separately assembled headlines, logos and actions.

**Copy treatment.** Prefer imagery that can combine with native text without duplication. Apply the selected profile’s overlay policy; any permitted embedded words must remain meaningful in multiple combinations.

**Scene and composition.** Create distinct landscape, square and portrait compositions. Preserve focal subjects through expected crop and assembly; clean product/context imagery should carry the meaning.

**Action and native fields.** Native CTA and destination govern action. Keep business name and logo assets separate where the campaign uses native brand controls; do not treat a flattened poster as the full asset group.

**Sequence and options.** Deliver the requested ratio/slot inventory with consistent SKU and proposition. Quantity recommendations never authorize extra generation. Check combinations, not only isolated files.

**Required inputs.** Campaign type, asset-group theme, required slots, native copy/logo assets and landing page. Required publisher fields remain separate from image production.

**What to review.** Check all required assets, native combinations, crops, consistency and each file’s constraints. A raster export does not prove account acceptance or campaign performance.

**What to avoid.** Avoid one resized poster everywhere, collages that become illegible when assembled, baked fake controls and mixing unrelated products into an asset group.

### Fixed-size display banner

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Communicate a single relevant proposition in a small, externally placed rectangle. Decide the minimum useful product cue, brand cue and action for each exact size.

**Copy treatment.** Use compact copy appropriate to the actual pixel height. At very short sizes, simplify the proposition rather than miniaturise the whole campaign. Essential qualifiers must still be readable or the claim must change.

**Scene and composition.** Re-layout wide, square and tall banners separately. Let product, headline and action have clear relative priorities; do not make tiny photoreal details responsible for recognition.

**Action and native fields.** Use one truthful action that the actual destination fulfils. Follow the selected profile on graphical controls; never simulate unavailable close/download/play functions.

**Sequence and options.** A banner set has one inventory row per requested size, all tied to one message and supplied offer; no silent omission of difficult small sizes.

**Required inputs.** Exact placement dimensions/file cap, campaign objective, destination, claim/offer terms and inventory list.

**What to review.** Inspect at 100% actual exported size, file weight, clipping, qualifier readability and completeness across sizes.

**What to avoid.** Avoid scaling down a social poster, unreadable legal lines and a universal font-size claim across physically different previews.

## Website

### Website desktop / mobile hero

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Orient the visitor and make the page promise clear. Decide whether the image demonstrates the product, establishes context or supports a live HTML proposition.

**Copy treatment.** Prefer live HTML for the headline, supporting text and button when the component supports it. Do not generate duplicate text inside the image; baked-copy requests require their own crop and readability checks.

**Scene and composition.** Plan desktop and mobile compositions around real component bounds and focal point. Use a separate mobile image when the desktop scene cannot survive cropping. Natural negative space must be intentional, not added haze.

**Action and native fields.** The site’s real button and destination carry the action. Reserve space for actual text overlays and avoid confusing image-based buttons with working controls.

**Sequence and options.** Treat desktop/mobile sources, focal coordinates, alt text and component usage as one responsive handoff. Preserve the same product and page promise across variants.

**Required inputs.** Actual component widths/heights, breakpoint/crop behaviour, live text/CTA placement, loading/performance budget and product source.

**What to review.** Render in the real component at laptop and mobile widths, with text wrapping and crop. Inspect focal preservation, overlap, alt purpose and actual bytes; standalone-image review is insufficient.

**What to avoid.** Avoid assuming one universal hero size, forcing desktop text into a mobile crop, depicting CTA as a decorative object or claiming source resolution from upscaled export dimensions.

### Website / app promotional banner

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Support a specific page task or campaign message without competing with page navigation and primary content.

**Copy treatment.** Use one concise proposition. Keep supplied price, dates and material terms together. Prefer native page text where available; secondary terms cannot disappear on mobile.

**Scene and composition.** Design for the banner’s actual displayed short height, real container crop and adjacent UI. Prioritise one recognisable product or visual motif.

**Action and native fields.** Use the component’s real click target and destination; state whether the whole banner or a native button is clickable.

**Sequence and options.** Desktop/mobile variants form one delivery set. If a narrow version cannot preserve the message and qualifier, change the layout or request an alternate component.

**Required inputs.** Component template, breakpoint sizes, baked-versus-live copy decision, destination and current brief offer terms.

**What to review.** Check smallest rendered instance, hover/focus styling owned by the site, crop and message consistency. Verify destination mapping separately from image appearance.

**What to avoid.** Avoid giant campaign headlines in shallow strips, unreadable footnotes, redundant buttons and using outdated website prices as brief input.

### Category tiles / landing-page modules

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Give each module a role in the page journey: navigate, demonstrate, explain, compare or reassure. Prioritise the user’s next decision, not a random collection of visuals.

**Copy treatment.** Tile labels should distinguish destinations. Explanation modules use concise headings and real evidence; long details belong in selectable page content.

**Scene and composition.** Keep visual scale and crop consistent across sibling tiles. Give each module a distinct visual job; art-direct with neighbouring modules and mobile stacking in mind.

**Action and native fields.** Map every tile/module to the intended native destination or action. Do not repeat a purchase CTA on every informational block.

**Sequence and options.** Outline the complete page sequence before creating assets. Review whether the sequence answers the brief’s main uncertainty and avoids repeating the hero.

**Required inputs.** Page wireframe, module IDs/order, product/category scope, responsive template and destinations.

**What to review.** Inspect within page context: sequence, mobile stacking, image/text pairing, crop, alt text and missing modules.

**What to avoid.** Avoid calling a folder of banners a landing page, using inconsistent product scale or filling unsupported modules with invented proof.

### Article / blog / Open Graph image

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Represent the specific article or announcement honestly and make it recognisable when shared outside the site.

**Copy treatment.** Use optional short title/context. The page title and social-preview text are separate; do not bake the entire article summary into the image.

### Retail screen still

Mapped to selected routes.

**Strategy and rationale.** Make one supplied product, offer or campaign idea understandable within the screen’s viewing opportunity. A passing shopper and a waiting shopper need different information density.

**Copy treatment.** In this image phase, do not invent headlines or buttons. If wording is supplied, its density follows dwell time and distance, with mandatory price/claim qualifiers retained. One focal message is a design preference, not a fixed word-count rejection rule.

**Scene and composition.** Recompose for the actual landscape, portrait or custom canvas rather than stretch. Landscape may separate subject and supporting area; portrait may stack them while protecting eye-level emphasis. Choose hierarchy from the brief, not a fixed template. Respect supplied safe insets, glare and screen obstructions. Unknown crop behaviour stays an explicit assumption.

**Action and native fields.** Make the action locally relevant: ask staff, inspect product or visit a supplied destination. Do not draw touchscreen controls on a passive display.

**Sequence and options.** If multiple stills are requested, each has a distinct role and can be understood when seen alone. Scheduling/rotation is a separate handoff.

**Required inputs.** Screen resolution/orientation, physical size, distance, active-area template, loop/dwell context and store-specific offer. Treat missing site/template data as an explicit planning assumption; continue independent image work. Do not invent measurements or demand repeated approval of accepted website/brief sources.

**What to review.** Check on the target screen or a calibrated representative preview, including distance readability, crop and complete loop inventory. Separate visible artwork defects from unknown installation evidence. Advisory density or viewing-distance advice alone must not fail the whole set. Scope any recovery to the affected image. Inspect the actual PNG/JPEG dimensions and supplied byte ceiling separately from visible quality. Fit, cover and stretch are distinct player behaviours; successful image export does not prove the player configuration.

**What to avoid.** Avoid portrait-to-landscape stretching, invented player limits, tiny required terms, mock interactive controls, and claiming a still contains motion, scheduling or audio.

## Training & sales

### Training deck / cheat sheet / mobile reference / FAQ

Defined — route connection pending.

**Strategy and rationale.** Help the learner perform one task or retrieve one buying answer. Choose teaching sequence for a deck, scanning for a cheat sheet, and rapid lookup for a phone card.

**Copy treatment.** Use exact supplied facts and procedures. Pair questions with direct answers; keep steps short without removing safety or product qualifications.

Defined — route connection pending.

**Strategy and rationale.** Help a salesperson answer a specified comparison using supplied subjects and criteria. Keep the comparison on hold when those inputs have not been supplied.
